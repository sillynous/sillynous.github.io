About the project

What is it for an act of judgment to be determinate — to settle a question, rather than merely land on one side of it? Frege locates the answer in content; the rejectivists (Smiley, Rumfitt, Price) distribute it across two primitive forces; Brandom grounds it in normative practice. I argue that these accounts share an assumption of act-extrinsic determinacy: each explains the exclusion of the opposite by something outside the act of judging. Against this, the dissertation develops an account of belief — judgment — on which exclusionary structure belongs to the act’s own self-conscious form.

“One and the Same”: Acceptance, Rejection, and the Unity of Judgment

Draft, July 2026

Abstract

An early Fregean fragment claims that rejecting a thought and accepting its opposite are one and the same. Frege’s later doctrine secures the identity only by reduction; Smiley, Rumfitt, Price, and Brandom recover successively stronger relations between rejection and asserted negation — equivalence, interderivability, dual description of one utterance, identity of deontic position — without explaining the identity itself. This paper argues that judgment is the determination of an issue whose opposed resolutions are already intelligible in understanding a thought: accepting p and rejecting ¬p are two descriptions of one answer — the first through what is affirmed, the second through what is ruled out.

The Ancient Cynics, and Their Discontents?

Draft as of February 2022 · unpublished

About

Were the Cynics philosophers, or just street theater? Against Ansgar Allen’s reading of them as unprincipled countercultural practice — no doctrine, no telos — this essay argues that the anecdotes, read patiently, show the Dogs to be tamer, funnier, and more principled than advertised: an articulable ethics of simple contentment, with reasons behind the shamelessness. Allen, it suggests, projects modern lowercase-‘c’ cynicism back onto the ancients.
